# Zau — Story Bible

The load-bearing document. Everything else — district flavor, NPC dialogue, side hooks — should be checked against this rather than invented independently, so nothing contradicts the actual history once it surfaces. Living doc, same as WORLD.md: argue with it, don't treat it as locked.

## The premise, in one paragraph

Zau's signature storms aren't weather. Years ago, Meridian Dynamics drilled into the pre-city tunnels beneath the Outskirts searching for a limitless power source, and found something instead — an ordinary wild Pokémon, transformed by whatever their excavation disturbed into something the company didn't understand and couldn't contain. They sealed the tunnels, buried the story, and called it a natural-disaster write-off. What they actually did was leave something alive down there, alone, growing for years, its instability bleeding upward through the city as thunder. Now Director Vance — who wasn't responsible for the original disaster, but has spent his career convinced he can finish it right — is reopening the excavation. The player's journey through Zau runs parallel to his, and ends at the same place: face to face with what Meridian made.

## Timeline

- **~18 years ago — The Incident.** A Meridian Dynamics research division, digging beneath what's now the Outskirts in search of a deep-earth energy source, breaches something that destabilizes a wild Pokémon in the excavation zone — a natural Grass-type, caught in whatever the drilling released. It doesn't die. It changes, badly, into what will eventually be known as Verdanyx. The site is evacuated. People are hurt; the official record blames equipment failure and a small number of "reckless" field staff, most notably an engineer named **Voss** — Dario's mother.
- **The cover-up.** Meridian seals the tunnels, quietly settles with or discredits the families of anyone who pushes back, and rebrands the incident out of public memory. A handful of people close to the project — including a young researcher named **Mabosso** — know the official story doesn't hold up, and leave rather than stay quiet about it.
- **The growing years.** Verdanyx doesn't stay still. It grows, and as it grows, Zau's storms get more frequent and more violent — a slow-burn consequence nobody outside Meridian connects to a sealed tunnel system nobody remembers. Meridian, meanwhile, never actually stopped being curious what they'd found.
- **Present day.** Director Vance, having risen through Meridian on the conviction that the original excavation was abandoned too early, is quietly reopening it — buying up land and tunnel access across the city (Cargo Row's unexplained shipping activity in Harbor is this: equipment and personnel moving toward the old site without moving through official channels). The game begins as this reaches its final stage, and the player's rise through the Wild Zone Trail and the League happens on the same clock as Vance's approach to the Underlight.

## What Verdanyx actually is

Not an ancient guardian, not something that was always down there waiting. It used to be a real, ordinary wild Pokémon before the Incident — the game doesn't need to specify which species it was before the transformation, and probably shouldn't; the point is that it was something small and unremarkable that Meridian's negligence turned into something neither natural nor stable. Grass/Dragon as its current typing reflects that instability — it's not a design that existed before this happened to it.

Its growing power, not malice, is what's destabilizing Zau's weather. It hasn't attacked the city. It's been alone in the dark for eighteen years, in pain, getting stronger without anyone trying to help it. This matters for how the endgame should feel: **the final confrontation is not "defeat the monster," it's "confront what Meridian made and decide what happens to it."** The existing end-screen text already says the player "uncovered the truth beneath the city" rather than "destroyed the threat" — that phrasing was accidentally exactly right, and the story should be written to earn it. Whether the actual ending is a cure, a release, a negotiated calming, or something else stays open for when Act 3 gets written in detail — but "just a superboss" is off the table now.

## Meridian Dynamics

A power/infrastructure conglomerate with a real, city-shaping footprint — this is why they plausibly control land near Signal District's tech infrastructure, Cargo Row's shipping, and the old transit tunnels all at once. Publicly, they're Zau's biggest employer and the reason the upper strata (Meridian Tower, the future Sprawl/Skyline) exist at all. The company as a whole isn't a monolithic evil — most of it is just people doing infrastructure work with no idea what happened under one old research division two decades ago.

## Director Vance — true believer, not a cartoon villain

Vance joined Meridian not long after the Incident, young enough to have heard the sanitized internal version of it as a cautionary tale rather than lived it. His whole career has been built on a conviction: *the original team gave up too soon, and their failure is the reason Zau still has an energy divide between the glass towers and everyone stacked beneath them.* He genuinely believes that if he can finish what they started — understand and safely harness whatever's down there — he ends the divide, ends the storms, and redeems a mistake the company has spent eighteen years pretending never happened.

He is wrong, and the story doesn't need to be shy about that — but he's wrong for a reason that makes him worth arguing with instead of just fighting. His blind spot is the line between *harness* and *exploit*: he's never once considered that the thing under the city might be a "who" and not a "what." That's the actual confrontation at Meridian Tower — not a mustache-twirling reveal, but Vance being genuinely convinced he's the hero of this story, right up until he isn't.

## Professor Mabosso

Was close enough to the original research division to know the official account of the Incident doesn't add up, and left rather than stay quiet — which is why "the last independent lab in Zau" is independent on purpose, not by circumstance. He doesn't hand the player a starter and send them into the Wild Zone out of pure tradition; he does it because he'd rather see an ordinary trainer, with no corporate stake in the outcome, be the one who eventually reaches the truth — instead of it staying Meridian's secret to manage. He likely knows more than he says early on, and has reasons (protecting Dario, mostly) not to lead with it.

## Dario Voss — the rival with something to prove

Dario's mother was the field engineer Meridian's official story blamed for the Incident. He grew up on that version of events — that she was careless, that she cost the company and possibly people their safety, that the family name needed rebuilding. He doesn't know it's a cover story. His drive as a rival was never really about beating the player; it's about being undeniably good enough that nobody can say a Voss doesn't belong in Zau.

**Arc across the acts:**
- **Act 1** — reads as a straightforward friendly-intense rival. This still works exactly as written; nothing here contradicts the existing Wild Zone Trail battle.
- **Act 2** — as the player rises through the League, Dario starts brushing up against Meridian directly (their money is everywhere a rising trainer would look for backing/sponsorship). He's tempted — Meridian rehabilitating his family's name is exactly what he's wanted his whole life, and they'd happily offer it in exchange for cooperation or silence.
- **Act 3** — Dario learns the real story, likely around the same time the player does, and has to choose between the vindication Meridian's willing to hand him and the truth about what actually happened to his mother (who, worth deciding later, may not even be dead — "discredited and disappeared" leaves room for her to still be out there). He should end up siding with the player against Vance, but it should cost him something to get there.

## Everyone else — flavor unless noted

Most of the cast doesn't need to carry plot weight, and shouldn't be forced to — that's how you end up with shallow everything instead of a few things that matter. Explicitly staying flavor: the 5 Act 1 trainers, and 4 of the 5 League Leaders.

One exception worth flagging: **Leader Obsidian** (Undercity Vault, Dark-type) runs her gym literally inside the stratum where the old tunnels are. She doesn't need a backstory overhaul, but she's a credible, natural source for undercity rumors and half-true stories about "something under the old lines" — a light lore touchpoint the player can bump into before Act 3 confirms what it actually was, not a major character.

## How this maps onto the acts already built

- **Act 1 (Wild Zone Trail → Dario)**: unaffected by any of this — it's the on-ramp, and Dario's introduction plays exactly as it does now. The groundwork above is for what his rematches/appearances start hinting at, later.
- **Act 2 (League)**: this is where Meridian's reach becomes visible district by district — Cargo Row in Harbor is the template; each district's eventual deep-dive can plant a similar small, specific "Meridian's doing something here they won't explain" hook rather than a repeated identical thread.
- **Act 3 (Vance → Verdanyx)**: the reveal. Vance's true-believer motivation, Dario's choice, and Verdanyx's real nature all land here. This act needs its own detailed pass later — this bible sets up what it has to deliver, not the scene-by-scene of how.

## Open threads (intentionally unresolved)

- Is Dario's mother still alive, and if so, where? Leaving this open on purpose.
- What was Verdanyx before the Incident? Deliberately unspecified — naming a real pre-transformation species risks the story leaning on a real Pokémon's established lore in ways that don't fit a custom, evolving legendary.
- What does "harnessing" Verdanyx would have even produced, mechanically, if Vance had succeeded? Not load-bearing for the story, can stay vague indefinitely.
