# Zau — Story Bible

The load-bearing document. Everything else — district flavor, NPC dialogue, side hooks — should be checked against this rather than invented independently, so nothing contradicts the actual history once it surfaces. Living doc, same as WORLD.md: argue with it, don't treat it as locked.

This is the plot machinery — who did what, when, why it matters, and **the full beat sheet of what the player actually does**. For who these people *are* — personality, voice — see [`CHARACTERS.md`](CHARACTERS.md). For how Mega Evolution fits the plot and where every stone comes from, see [`MEGA.md`](MEGA.md). For optional content, [`SIDEQUESTS.md`](SIDEQUESTS.md).

## The premise, in one paragraph

Zau's signature storms aren't weather. Eighteen years ago, Meridian Dynamics drilled into the pre-city tunnels beneath the Outskirts searching for a limitless power source, and found one — a deep-earth energy that changes living things. It transformed an ordinary wild Pokémon caught in the excavation into something the company didn't understand and couldn't contain. They sealed the tunnels, buried the story, and blamed a field engineer. What they actually did was leave something alive down there, alone, in pain, growing, its instability bleeding upward through the city as thunder. Now Director Vance — who wasn't responsible for the original disaster, but has spent his career convinced he can finish it right — is reopening the excavation. The player's journey through Zau runs on the same clock as his, and ends at the same place: face to face with what Meridian made, holding the one thing Meridian never understood about the energy they found — that it answers to a bond.

## The energy (this is what the whole plot is about)

Meridian's research division called it **deep resonance**. It's the same energy that, elsewhere in the world, formed Mega Stones and Key Stones: a force that can push a Pokémon past its natural form. The real games are explicit that Mega Evolution only works through the bond between a trainer and a Pokémon, and reverts when the battle ends. That's the thematic spine of Zau:

- **Mega Evolution** is deep resonance *with* a bond, *with* a revert. Temporary, consensual, held together by two beings trusting each other.
- **Verdanyx** is deep resonance *without* either. A wild Pokémon, alone, transformed with no one to hold it and no way back. It has been "Mega Evolved" for eighteen years with nothing to revert to.

So the game's signature mechanic isn't a gimmick bolted onto the story — it's the answer to the story's central problem, and the player learns the mechanic at exactly the moment the plot needs them to understand it.

## Timeline

- **~18 years ago — The Incident.** Meridian's research division, digging beneath what's now the Outskirts, breaches a deep-resonance seam. The lead field engineer, **Elena Voss**, orders the drill stopped; her division head overrules her and keeps drilling. The seam vents. A wild Grass-type in the excavation zone is caught in it and transformed — into what will eventually be called Verdanyx. The site is evacuated; people are hurt. The official record blames "equipment failure and reckless field staff," Elena Voss by name.
- **The cover-up.** Meridian seals the tunnels, settles with or discredits the families who push back, and rebrands the whole thing out of public memory. **Mabosso**, a young researcher on the division, leaves rather than sign the official account. Elena Voss is fired, publicly blamed, and — within a year — gone. The story everyone in the Outskirts knows is that she left her husband and infant son and vanished.
- **What actually happened to Elena.** She went *down*. She was the only person who'd seen what the seam did up close, and she couldn't leave it alone in the dark. She found a way into the old lines beneath the Undercity and has spent eighteen years there, keeping Verdanyx as calm as one person can — not taming it, not fighting it, just being *there*. The Undercity knows her only as **the Warden**. Her Absol is the one thing that comes and goes between her world and the city above.
- **The growing years.** Verdanyx doesn't stay still. It grows, and the storms grow with it. Meridian, meanwhile, never stopped being curious what they'd found.
- **Present day.** Director Vance, having risen through Meridian on the conviction that the original excavation was abandoned too early, is quietly reopening it — buying tunnel access, moving equipment through Harbor's Cargo Row, pulling power through the Ember Quarter's substation, drawing water samples through the Greenline's pumps. The game begins as this reaches its final stage.

## What Verdanyx actually is

Not an ancient guardian, not something that was always down there waiting. It used to be a real, ordinary wild Pokémon — the game never specifies which, and shouldn't. Grass/Dragon as its typing reflects the instability of what was done to it. Its growing power, not malice, is what's destabilising Zau's weather. It hasn't attacked the city. It's been alone in the dark for eighteen years, in pain, getting stronger, with one human keeping it company.

**The final confrontation is not "defeat the monster," it's "confront what Meridian made and give it what it never had."** See the Act 3 beats for exactly how that plays.

## Structure at a glance

| Act | Where | Badges | Team level band | What it's about |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 — The Outskirts | Home, Outskirts, Wild Zone Trail | — | 5–18 | Learning to be a trainer. Dario. The storms are background. |
| Act 2 — The Stack | Harbor → Ember → Greenline → *(Key Stone)* → Signal → Undercity | 1–5 | 18–35 | Meridian's reach, district by district. Each district's hook points down. Ends with the truth. |
| Act 3 — The Descent | The Sprawl → Meridian Tower → the Undercity again → the Underlight | — | 35–48 | Vance. Dario's choice. Verdanyx. |
| Postgame — The Skyline | Above the tower | — | 50+ | Rematches, the Skyline, Verdanyx at rest, water travel. |

Real Pokémon games pace roughly one main-story beat *between* every gym, not just the gym — that's the rule here. Every district has at least one thing that happens in it that isn't the badge.

## ACT 1 — The Outskirts

*Already built. Recorded here so the later acts know what they're paying off.*

1. **Home.** Alma sends the player off with something repaired, not bought (a mended old Bag — the reason the player's Bag has more slots than a store-bought one, if that ever becomes mechanical). She mentions the storms have been worse this year.
2. **Mabosso's lab.** Starter choice. Priya is picking hers at the same time. Mabosso is warm about Pokémon and clipped about anything else.
3. **The Wild Zone Trail.** Five trainers (Reyes, Dana, Milo, Talia, Zeke), each with one real trait. Priya waits at the trail's end.
4. **Dario.** The rival intro. Loud, competitive, checks his team's items three times before the battle. Winning gets the player the Trail's clearance — nothing to do with Key Stones (that's moved; see below).
5. **Act 1 close.** The Harbor Steps open. Alma calls: "the storm last night took the roof off the Underpass depot. That's never happened."

## ACT 2 — The Stack

Each district: arrive, meet its people, one main-story thread that isn't the gym, the gym. The three built districts already have their hooks — cargo, power, water — and this act is where they stop being oddities and start being one operation.

### Harbor District — *cargo* (Badge 1: Coral)

- **H1 — Arrival.** Harbormaster Rossi at the boarded Ferry Terminal: boats used to leave; they stopped; nobody official will say why. The Fish Market. Keeper Ambrose at the Lighthouse tells the player Zau was *down* before it was *up* — the first mention that there's something under the city that predates it.
- **H2 — Dario is already here.** He's been watching Cargo Row for two days. He's not investigating Meridian; he's *interested* in them — they're the biggest sponsor a trainer could get. But even he thinks the trucks are weird. Rival battle 2.
- **H3 — Cargo Row, after dark.** Maren (the off-duty dockworker, at the Market) tells the player the crates are stencilled with dig codes she remembers from her father's time. Dario talks the player into the yard at night. Inside: excavation gear, crates marked for a site "L-0". **Meridian Site Security** (a new trainer class — polite, corporate, wearing the logo) chases them out. First time the player battles Meridian.
- **Gym.** Coral, playing to the empty tanks.
- **Leaving.** Priya, at the Lighthouse, has been listening to Ambrose. "He says the storms used to come from the sea. They don't anymore. They come from *under*."

### Ember Quarter — *power* (Badge 2: Ashgrave)

- **E1 — Brownouts.** Foreman Kettering at the Kilns: the Quarter draws less power than it did ten years ago and the lights go out every night. The substation went in three months ago.
- **E2 — Halloran.** Dr. Ines Halloran, Meridian's community-relations director, is at the Scrapyard Exchange being *nice* — funding the Exchange's repairs, sponsoring local trainers. She singles Dario out. She knows his surname and says it warmly. This is the first person who has ever done that. (Halloran is not a villain in disguise; see CHARACTERS.md. She's the human face of a company that is doing a terrible thing without most of its people knowing.)
- **E3 — The blackout.** The Quarter goes fully dark mid-conversation. Kettering needs the mill line restarted or the furnace cracks. **The Boiler Tunnels** — the game's first real cave/dungeon, under the Kilns: wild Fire/Rock encounters, a couple of off-duty mill workers as trainers, and at the end, the substation's trunk cable, running *down* into a sealed service tunnel with a Meridian keycard door. The player restores the line. Kettering: "So who's drawing?"
- **Gym.** Ashgrave, who doesn't stop the furnace for anyone.
- **Leaving.** Dario has accepted Halloran's sponsorship. He's wearing the pin. He's happy about it, and the player isn't in a position to say why they shouldn't be.

### Greenline Terraces — *water* (Badge 3: Thistle)

- **G1 — Old Sato.** The plants near the pump intake grow wrong. He stopped planting there. Nobody asked why, so he stopped saying.
- **G2 — Priya's samples.** Priya takes cuttings from the intake ferns. She also has notes: a white Pokémon with a curved horn has been seen on the Overlook before every storm this year. An **Absol** — the disaster Pokémon, the one that appears before calamity. Sato has seen it too. "It looks at the Outskirts. Then it looks *down*."
- **G3 — The storm.** A storm breaks over the terraces while the player is on the Overlook — the game's first real on-map cutscene: rain, lightning over the Outskirts far below, the Absol standing on the terrace edge watching the player. It leaves. Priya: "That wasn't a wild Pokémon. That was a *message*."
- **Gym.** Thistle, in the glasshouse, talking to their Floragato.

### The Key Stone — *between Badge 3 and Signal District*

This is the beat the whole first half builds to. Team level ~28–32.

- **K1 — The Absol returns.** After Thistle, the Absol is waiting at the Ember Quarter's sealed service tunnel (the door from E3). It has a **Meridian keycard** in its mouth — an old one, eighteen years old. The door opens for it.
- **K2 — The cache.** Behind the door, a short way into the old lines: a supply cache from the original excavation. A field engineer's locker, name plate: **E. VOSS**. Inside, a Key Stone on a cord, an Absolite, and a note in a hand that's been waiting a long time — addressed to no one, or to whoever the Absol chose to bring. The gist: *"If you're reading this, it sent you. It only brings people who can be trusted with it. Take the stone. Learn what it does. Then come find me. — E.V."*
- **K3 — The gift.** The Absol chooses the player. It joins the party — **a story gift Pokémon**, high level for its moment (Lv.30), *with genuinely excellent stats*: see MEGA.md on IVs (a real IV system is the mechanical way to make "good IVs" true instead of flavor; until it exists, the gift Absol is built with hand-authored above-curve stats). It holds the Absolite. This is deliberately a Mega-eligible Pokémon so that every player, whatever they've caught, has a Mega available when the plot needs one.
- **K4 — Mabosso explains.** Back at the lab (or by call), Mabosso finally talks. Deep resonance. Mega Evolution. The bond, the revert. And — for the first time — that he was *there*, and that E. Voss was the engineer who tried to stop the drill. He doesn't tell the player she's alive; he doesn't know for certain. He hasn't told Dario any of this, and asks the player not to yet.
- **K5 — The first Mega.** The player Mega Evolves in the next real battle. The tutorial is the plot.

### Signal District — *signal* (Badge 4: Prism)

- **S1 — Prism's problem.** Leader Prism runs Signal Tower as a lab. The storms have an electrical signature that matches no weather pattern in any record. Prism doesn't care about Meridian; Prism cares that the data doesn't make sense.
- **S2 — The server farm.** Meridian's data centre is the district's biggest tenant. Priya and Prism, with the player running the errands (a small dungeon in the tower's cable risers, Electric/Steel wilds, Meridian technicians as trainers), pull the storm signature and clean it up. It's rhythmic. Slow. Prism: "That's not weather. That's a *pulse*."
- **S3 — Dario, sponsored.** Rival battle 3. He's in Meridian colours, Halloran on the sidelines. He's better than he's ever been. He's also visibly not sure why the player looks at him like that. **If the player has Mega Evolved, this is where Dario sees it for the first time.** He wants one. Halloran, quietly, would very much like to be the one who gives it to him.
- **Gym.** Prism, for whom losing is data.

### The Undercity — *the truth* (Badge 5: Obsidian)

Getting down is its own beat: the **Drowned Stair** in the Harbor is flooded, and draining it is a job for Rossi's old ferry pumps and Kettering's crew — a small cross-district quest that pays off two districts' placeholder spots at once.

- **U1 — Descent.** The Drowned Stair opens into the Old Lines — Zau's forgotten transit tunnels, the game's biggest dungeon so far. Ghost/Dark/Poison wilds, tunnel-dweller trainers, and the first *shrines*: places where something has been kept company, for a long time — old blankets, a kettle, chalk marks counting years.
- **U2 — Obsidian.** The Undercity Vault gym is a converted bank vault. Obsidian has seen stranger things than a trainer walking in. Rumours: "the Warden." Someone has lived down here longer than anyone, and she's the reason the deep tunnels are *quiet*. Obsidian won't say more until after the badge. Gym.
- **U3 — The Warden.** Past the Vault, down: Elena Voss. She's been expecting the Absol's choice for years. She tells the player everything — the seam, the drill, the transformation, why she stayed. That Verdanyx is not a monster; it's a Pokémon that has been in mid-transformation for eighteen years with no one on the other end of the bond. That she's kept it calm but it's *growing*, and that Vance's rig — which she can hear now, through the rock — will wake it fully. She asks about her son. The player has to decide whether to tell Dario.
- **Act 2 close.** Vance activates the first stage of the rig. The biggest storm yet. The Old Lines shake. Elena: "Go up. Find whoever's doing this. And bring my son, if he'll come."

## ACT 3 — The Descent

### The Sprawl — *the choice*

The Sprawl is Meridian's public face — the mid-city where the company is a landlord, an employer, a sponsor. No gym. Two things happen:

- **SP1 — Dario.** The player finds him at Halloran's community office, about to sign a longer contract: Meridian will fund a public review "clearing the Voss family name." It's real — Halloran means it, and Vance approved it, because a grateful, famous young Voss is exactly the story Meridian wants told when the excavation goes public as a success. The player tells him his mother is alive and where. He doesn't believe it. Rival battle 4 — the angriest one. He loses. He goes quiet and short, the way he does when something lands. He leaves. He doesn't say where.
- **SP2 — Halloran.** Alone, Halloran asks the player what they said to him. The player tells her. She didn't know. It's on her face that she didn't know. She gives the player a Meridian Tower access badge and doesn't say why.

### Meridian Tower — *Vance*

- **T1 — The lobby, the elevator, the floors.** Site Security trainers, executives, the company as a place where most people are just doing infrastructure work. The thing on Vance's desk from the original excavation: it's a drill-bit fragment, resonance-scarred, that glows very faintly when the player's Key Stone is near it.
- **T2 — Vance.** Not a reveal — a *conversation*. He knows who the player is. He believes he is ending the energy divide, ending the storms, redeeming a mistake the company spent eighteen years pretending never happened. He is wrong for a reason worth arguing with. The player says the word "who." He says the word "what." Battle. He loses.
- **T3 — He does it anyway.** Losing a Pokémon battle doesn't stop a man with a rig and a conviction. He activates the full sequence from the desk. The Tower's windows go white. "If you're right about it," he says, "then go and be right. I'll be here when the lights come back."

### The Underlight — *Verdanyx*

- **V1 — The way down.** The Tower's freight core drops the player to the Undercity in one long elevator cutscene — the whole stack passing by, every district's lights flickering. At the bottom, the Old Lines are lit for the first time: Meridian's rig, running.
- **V2 — Dario.** He's there. He found her. Mother and son, eighteen years, with a legendary waking up in the next chamber — the scene is short because there's no time, and that's right. Elena: "Later. Go." Dario, to the player: "Bet you can't do this without me." He's not carrying Meridian's pin anymore. **Dario's Pokémon can Mega Evolve from here** (Halloran gave him a stone, once — it turns out she meant it as a gift, not a leash).
- **V3 — Verdanyx.** The battle. It's a Pokémon in agony with the rig driving it, and the fight is *stopping the rig* — Priya and Elena at the controls while the player holds it. Vance's rig fails. The Verdanyx that's left is exhausted, enormous, and still transformed.
- **V4 — The revert.** The ending mechanism is the mechanic the player learned in K4: a Key Stone bond is what a Mega has that Verdanyx never did. Elena has been the closest thing to a bond it's ever had. With her Key Stone (the player brought it back to her — she gave it away; the player gives it back), she reaches for it and it *reverts* — not into what it was, that's gone, but into a stable form. The storms stop. Not "defeated." **Held.**
- **Ending.** The truth surfaces (Prism's data, Priya's samples, Halloran's testimony, Elena alive). Vance resigns rather than lie about it — the one honest thing he does. The Voss name is cleared by the truth instead of by Meridian's press release. Alma gets a phone call from the bottom of the city. Credits. The Ferry Terminal opens.

## POSTGAME — The Skyline

- The Skyline opens (above the Tower): weather stations, sky bridges, the League's real elite as rematches with full teams and Megas.
- **Verdanyx** rests in the Skyline's highest weather station, where the storms used to form. It can be encountered and — with Elena's blessing and a real fight — caught. Its stable form is the one the player battles.
- Water travel: the ferry runs; where it goes is the next expansion's question.
- Trainer rematches, gym leader rematches with Megas, Dario as a recurring rival with a full Mega team.
