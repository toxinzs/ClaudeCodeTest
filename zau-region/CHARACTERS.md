# Zau — Characters

Companion to STORY.md. That file is the plot machinery; this one is the people running it. A character existing here doesn't mean they need a coded NPC yet — it means that when they do get written, dialogue, or a sprite, this is who they actually are, not a blank slot with a name on it.

## The player's circle

### Alma — the player's mother

Runs a repair shop out of the ground floor of their building in the Outskirts, keeping the old underpass transit cars running — the ones that predate Meridian, that Meridian has no interest in maintaining because there's no profit in them. She's good at it, proud of it, and pointedly doesn't work for the company, not out of politics exactly, just because she likes fixing things with her hands and doesn't answer to people who've never touched a wrench.

She's the one who signs off on the player heading into the Wild Zone — not thrilled about it, but she knows better than to try to stop a kid who's made up their mind, and there's a version of this where she did the same thing herself at that age. She worries about the storms more than she says out loud; she's old enough to remember them being milder, and young enough that it unsettles her that she can't say why they've changed. Sends the player off with something practical, not sentimental — a repaired old item, maybe, over anything store-bought.

**Voice**: direct, a little dry, doesn't do long goodbyes. Shows she cares through logistics ("you got your bag, you got your balls, you got a way to call me") rather than saying it outright.

### Priya — the friend who's coming too

Grew up two floors down from the player, in and out of each other's homes their whole lives. Gets a starter from Mabosso around the same time the player does, but she's not chasing gym badges — she wants to study Pokémon, not battle them competitively, and is only heading into the Wild Zone because it's the only way anyone gets anywhere in Zau. Where Dario measures himself against the player, Priya's just glad to not be doing this alone.

She shows up periodically rather than traveling at the player's shoulder constantly — waiting at a milestone (end of the Wild Zone Trail, somewhere in the League) with notes on something she's noticed, half a joke, occasionally healing items she's traded someone out of. She's the one who'd clock that wild Pokémon behavior near the Undercity has been changing before anyone official admits there's a reason — not because she's investigating Meridian, just because she actually watches the Pokémon she meets instead of only battling them. That instinct is what eventually puts her in the same room as the real story, without her ever setting out to find it.

**Voice**: curious, talks fast when excited, undercuts her own enthusiasm with self-deprecating jokes. Good listener when it isn't about Pokémon.

## The rival

### Dario Voss

What was written before was his backstory and his arc — not who he actually is to talk to. Fixing that:

He's loud about his confidence in a way that's clearly compensating, and everyone around him mostly knows it, including — on his worse days — him. He double- and triple-checks his team's condition and item loadout before every battle, obsessively, even ones that don't matter; it reads as competence but it's actually anxiety wearing competence as a costume. He's inherited his mother's habit of taking machines apart to see how they work, and still does it with old junk electronics when he's stressed, which is the one tell that the confident-rival performance has a person underneath it who's still just a kid whose family got publicly blamed for something before he was old enough to understand what.

He's not actually cruel, and it shows in small moments he'd deny if called out on them — he'll wait for the player to heal up before a rematch rather than press an advantage, he'll compliment a genuinely good play even mid-loss, he checks in (badly, sideways, never directly) after the player's had a rough battle elsewhere. His real flaw isn't arrogance, it's that he doesn't know how to want something quietly — everything has to be proven, publicly, immediately, because the alternative is sitting with the doubt.

**Arc, now with a person attached to it**: Act 1 plays exactly as built — the loud, competitive rival intro. Act 2, Meridian's outreach to him isn't a boardroom offer, it's smaller and more human — someone from the company being kind to him, treating him like a Voss is worth investing in, which is exactly the thing he's never gotten and exactly why it works on him for a while. Act 3's choice costs him something real: turning down the first people who ever offered to publicly clear his mother's name, on the word of a stranger (the player) who's telling him that offer was never honest.

**Voice**: talks in challenges even when he doesn't mean them as one ("bet your Sprigatito can't—"), goes quiet and short when something actually lands emotionally instead of getting louder.

## The antagonists

### Director Vance

Physically unremarkable on purpose — the kind of exec who doesn't look like a threat until he's already three moves ahead. Speaks like someone who's rehearsed being reasonable, because he has: measured, never raises his voice, treats disagreement as a puzzle to be solved rather than a conflict to win, which is more unsettling than a shouting villain would be. Keeps something from the original excavation on his desk — a piece of equipment, a photo, something — that he's never explained to anyone at the company, because it's the closest thing he has to a reason he believes what he believes.

He is capable of being genuinely kind to people he's decided are useful to his goal — see: Dario — and it isn't fully performance. That's what makes him dangerous rather than just wrong: he'd tell you, honestly, that he thinks about the people at the bottom of the stack every day. He does. He's just decided the way to help them runs directly through something that doesn't get a vote in the matter.

**Voice**: calm, precise, occasionally lets a real flicker of the true-believer intensity through when the excavation itself comes up — the one topic where the composure cracks slightly.

## Mentors

### Professor Mabosso

Older than the "professor hands out starters" archetype usually implies — visibly been doing this a while, visibly tired in a way that isn't about age. Ran with the original Meridian research division young enough to believe in it, left old enough to know exactly what he was walking away from and what it cost him professionally. Doesn't lecture the player about any of this early on — deflects, changes the subject, is warmer about Pokémon care than about answering direct questions regarding his own history.

Genuinely likes his work independent of the larger stakes — he'd be doing this even without a slumbering company secret underneath the city, and that's part of why the player should trust him: his care for new trainers isn't a means to an end.

**Voice**: warm, a little rambling when it's actually about Pokémon, noticeably clipped and careful the moment a question edges toward Meridian's history.

## The wider cast — flavor, given real color instead of just names

Not plot-load-bearing, per STORY.md — but "not plot-relevant" shouldn't mean "cardboard." One real trait each, enough that they read as a person for the one scene they're in:

- **Camper Reyes** — insists on quoting outdoor-survival advice that's clearly made up on the spot.
- **Cyclist Dana** — narrates her own battles in third person, mid-fight, like a sports broadcast.
- **Skater Milo** — genuinely can't sit still; even his dialogue boxes should feel like he's mid-motion.
- **Busker Talia** — answers most questions with whatever song lyric feels closest, whether or not it fits.
- **Courier Zeke** — perpetually running late to something unrelated to the battle, treats it as a quick detour.
- **Leader Coral** — treats every battle like a performance for the aquarium's old viewing tanks, playing to a crowd that isn't there anymore.
- **Leader Ashgrave** — blunt, impatient with small talk, visibly softens the moment the battle's actually good.
- **Leader Thistle** — talks to their own Pokémon mid-battle more than to the player.
- **Leader Prism** — treats the gym like a lab experiment; losing visibly interests them as data, not just a loss.
- **Leader Obsidian** — the one exception already flagged in STORY.md for lore purposes; personality-wise, dry, unbothered, has clearly seen stranger things in the Undercity than a trainer walking in.
